## Hook (first 1-3 s)
- Open mid-action on a situation every owner recognises; the premise lands in one line (pilot: "Two plumbers. Both asleep. Only one gets paid.").
- The situation belongs to an owner and a customer, never to Ross himself.
- Write three hook variants and commit to the strongest; open on a pattern break.

## The middle
- A new shot every 1-3 s; no two similar angles back to back; a reverse shot for a reveal.
- One idea per beat; cut any beat that does not move the story; beats of 1.5-3.5 s.
- Running gags pay off when repeated with a twist (pilot: "very busy" / "His phone isn't.").

## Payoff and ending
- End on recognition, not on an instruction, a scolding or a moral. The twist is the lesson; never add a moral line.
- Land the reveal on the word or picture that names it, then call back an earlier prop.
- Episodes are judged by shares, follows and profile visits, not views. No handle and no logo (Ross 10-20: "no branding, just on the topic"), and since 10-23 no share line in the video either (Ross: "remove the send"; docs/format.md); any ask goes in the caption.

## Dialogue
- Short lines with a double meaning work (ep. 2: "Dave's Plumbing, how fast can I come?" / "Never been a problem for you.").
- End a scene on a short deadpan button (Barb: "Mm-hm.").
- The other character says the conclusion; nobody announces a rule; the last line is nearly empty.
- Cut speech-like cadence, antithesis ("not X, but Y"), runs of three-word fragments, "this is about" openers, hype words, em dashes.
- Read every line aloud; vary length; use contractions; use owners' real wording (reviews, forums), never a line a real person would not say.
- Wit: an angry truth, an unexpected image, a dry deadpan button. Swear on the spike only.
- Never soften a character with explanations.

## The series about how they sell to Denise (Ross 2026-10-21)
Since 10-22 this sketch is the whole episode (docs/format.md); the true story, Rhonda's question and the outro are gone.
- **Shape.** Every episode is a purchase. Denise tries to buy one thing, and a seller, an app or a screen pushes something on her with one real technique from Cialdini's "Influence". Ross: "the main character must always be trying to buy something, and something gets pushed on him, or something is offered to her". A place with nothing to buy has nothing to do with marketing; he turned down the restroom line for that reason.
- **Ending.** Her friend Rhonda asks why she did it, and Denise gets it: "Fucking marketing." Ross: "so it's clear what's what".
- **No lecture.** No history, study or moral in the video; a real fact can go in the caption (Ross 10-22: "funny videos that have gone off the rails... not stuffy ones").
- **Escalation.** Each upsell is bigger than the last: it starts with the ones every counter uses and ends somewhere unhinged. Ross 10-21: "the punchlines have to keep building", "exaggerated and just fucking off the rails".
- **Pace.** Ross 10-21: "sharper, bolder and more dynamic - the scenes are too long". Scenes of a few seconds, no long silent beats; the whole episode runs 20–30 s. A repeated beat gets faster each time.

## Staging
- Before each frame: who looks where; light from a real source (a TV's glow on the faces watching it); objects face their user; the camera stands where a camera could.
- One palette, one focal point, fewer shapes.
- For each shot, list each object with its count and position, and say what it must not become.
