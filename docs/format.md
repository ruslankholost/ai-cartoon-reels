# The format of the series (Ross 2026-10-22)

Ross 10-22, after "I'm Just Supporting a Friend" v1: "the video came out total bullshit - in short, from now on post funny videos that have gone off the rails, not these stuffy ones". The wolf format of 10-21 (belief → "Who told you that?" → a sourced true story → sketch → moral outro) is dropped; it is in the original repo's history (not in this copy). This file wins over docs/reel-rules.md and the screenwriting skill where they disagree.

## Why
- Our best reel is still the dumbest one: "Friday Night Lead", 5.1k views, about 62% watched to the end. Frame 1 was a woman in a snorkel mask on a flamingo float in her flooded basement.
- The history episodes played as lectures. "Not a Sales Person" v2 got skipped a lot (Ross 10-22), and he called "Supporting a Friend" (64 s, a sourced story in sepia) "total bullshit".

## An episode
- One sketch, 20–30 s, no shot over 2.4 s.
- The first 3 seconds follow the next section.
- A ladder (Ross 10-21: "the absurdity from start to finish should only keep growing... exaggerated and just completely fucking off the rails"): it opens on something everyone has lived through, each punch is bigger than the last and follows from it, and it ends off the rails. Fewer, bigger rungs: five fast gags in a row land as none (test room 10-22).
- A turn at the end: a reversal or a callback to frame 1, not more of the same.
- The peak gives what the line before it asks for, in plain sense. In "Cold Lips" Todd shouts "MA'AM! ANYTHING!" to please her, so she asks for a foot rub. v7 had her say "Then turn off the internet.", which went after her threat to post and had nothing to do with "anything" (Ross 10-23: "super stupid bullshit, switching off the internet... 'anything' should please her - let it be at least a foot massage").
- The ending stays open, so the reel gets watched again (Ross 10-23: "the ending should be open, to provoke a rewatch"). The last shot leads back into frame one, or leaves a question that frame one answers. Example: in "Cold Lips" v8 she sips "the usual", her face snaps purple as in the opening, she winds up to throw, and the reel loops into the wall of coffee. An unclear last look (v5: just her wide eyes) does not work: the viewers could not tell what it meant and left.
- Expression in every beat, not only in the hook (Ross 10-23: "add more expression to the script"): big reactions, physical consequences, lines at full volume where the scene allows. A deadpan character still shows it, with a twitching eye or a throbbing vein, and gets one explosion. Ross himself no longer explodes (10-27, docs/bible.md): the others carry the volume.
- Topics (Ross 10-24): the series is about Ross, his made-up days as the owner of GetPhantom: something a company does to its customers sets him off, he sees what drives them away, and it builds until he blows (docs/bible.md, "The main character"). Before 10-24: anything everyone has lived through as a buyer, a worker or a customer. In a purchase episode Denise buys, Rhonda asks why, and Denise says "Fucking marketing." (Ross 10-21). Keep that line only where it makes sense in the scene. Later on 10-24 Ross opened it up: he can play any role, and the topic only has to be something a wide audience knows (docs/bible.md). On 10-27 he narrowed it again: the plane seat swap (x17) had nothing to do with small business ("I didn't get at all how this has anything to do with small business, hello?"), so a topic is about small business and still readable for a wide audience.
- No share line in the video (Ross 10-23: "remove the send"). The open ending loops, a line over the last shot tells the viewer it is over, and the test viewers read "Send this to..." as a brand's prompt. Any ask goes in the caption.

## The first 3 seconds (Ross 10-23: "the first frame matters", "the rules of the first three seconds have to change")
Ross 10-23: "The hook in the first three seconds has to be super eye-catching, sharp, loud, visually bold". A faster cut of the same beats is not a new hook: my first plan for a new "Cold Lips" opening kept the cup, the dog's face and "This is hot." / "It's coffee." and only cut them quicker, and he said "by the script it's the same thing". No trial reels on this account yet (Ross: they come at 200 followers), so a new cut goes out as a normal post.
Frame by frame, "Friday Night Lead" is the only reel where something happens in the first half second: the pipe bursts at 0.3 s, Linda floats in the snorkel mask at 0.8 s, the phone types "plumber near me" at 1.6 s. That is five pictures in 3 s; about 15% were gone by 3 s and it reached 2,869. Every other reel holds one shot of a character saying a line for 1.2-2.4 s, and they reached 115-1,420. Four test viewers saw the 0-2.8 s strips blind on 10-23: all four kept watching FNL, and all four left "Meet the Family" and "Cold Lips" between 0.8 and 2.4 s. "Cold Lips" is still the funniest script; its opening is what lost them.
- 0-0.5 s: something happens on a sound: a burst, a crash, a face snapping from calm to fury. A character standing there saying a line is not an opening.
- By 1 s: the oddest picture of the opening, big and bright. No dark frame, no wide shot with small figures.
- 0-3 s: a new picture every 0.4-1 s, in one place with one character and close-ups, so nobody has to find their way around. The calm staging starts after that.
- It reads with the sound off: whatever the line is about is big on screen (the viewers didn't notice the "Cold Lips" cup), and the line is short.
- The first 3 s leave a question open. "This is hot." / "It's coffee." finished a joke by 2.4 s, and the viewers left there. No introduction, no brand, no app screen first: an app screen as the opener reads as an ad, after the picture it works.
- Check: before the script goes to Ross, write the first 3 s as pictures only, with no lines. Once there is a cut, `$PY_IMG -I scripts/first3-strip.py out/<ep>.mp4 out/<ep>-first3.jpg` and show the strip to the four viewers. They called the best and the worst right but three of four kept "Not a Sales Person" (115 reached): a strip shows its answer at 2.0 s at once, and a real scroller is gone by then. A dark or still first second fails whatever they say.
- After 24 h compare the skip rate in the reel's Insights in the app.

## Out of the video
- History, studies, years, sums and sources; sepia story parts.
- "Who told you that?", "Shut up and look." and the outro "Either you're selling, or you're buying."
- Naming the trick: the picture shows it.

## Words
- About 40–60 words: lines a real person says at a counter, or what a real screen says. Every key beat is said aloud or shown big.
- Read every line for plain sense in its scene (Ross 10-22 on a fixed line: "what is this bullshit").
- The anti-AI line rules of docs/bible.md apply. Made-up names, no real brands or people.

## Picture
- The approved South Park puppets. Staging stays easy to read (one place, few faces, no stacked effects; Ross 10-20 "too chaotic"), and the expression comes from the acting, the events and the lines (Ross 10-23).
- Key text stays out of Instagram's bottom 400 px.
- The cover is frame 1's picture with the problem in big text.
- On TikTok the cover is the file's first frame (10-30). We post through GoHighLevel (GHL), which has no TikTok cover field, and TikTok's API takes only a frame and falls back to frame 0. x20 went out with its first frame, an empty counter, as the cover, and Ross noticed ("it posted but without a cover"). So the reel's very first frame is the cover: the episode's oddest picture with the label big on it. The opening shot starts on that picture instead of an empty set.

## How an episode starts
Ross gives a topic, and we push it as far as irony, absurdity and humor will go, then write a script that hits hard. In his words (10-22): "I'll throw out some topic, we take it to the apex of irony, absurdity and humor - and make a tooth-crushing script". The funniest one so far is x06 "Cold Lips" (Ross: "to me it's the funniest reel"). It sets the bar, but don't copy its structure. Each topic finds its own way to go off the rails (Ross: "it doesn't have to be literally like that").
- An action seen in profile needs side-view art. In Cold Lips v1 the front-only puppets made the kiss look like a kiss on her hip.

## The caption
- Ross's own point, in plain words, for the owner watching. Then an ask for stories with a light tease. Cold Lips: "Every service business has a customer like her. Groveling won't get you a better review, so don't. / Ask the customers who like you for a review instead. Enough good ones and hers gets buried. / Drop the wildest customer story you've seen or heard 👇 / Can't think of one? Maybe you're the main character."
- From the test room (10-22): the word "dignity" read as a lecture in every form. A tease must not call the owner the bad customer, since owners are the buyers. Anything after the first two lines sits below Instagram's "more".
- A real fact with its source may follow. It never goes into the video.

## The test room
Four Haiku readers get only the script (screenwriting skill, section 9): the Viewer, the Scroller, the Skeptic, the Overthinker. Since x19 a fifth reader joins them, from the other side of the argument (prompts/test-room.md has all five). Ask each where they laughed and to say the end joke in one sentence. They show whether a beat reads and where they would leave. They do not choose the peak: on 10-23 they ranked "Then turn off the internet." first of five, and Ross called it "super stupid bullshit".

## Judging an episode
After 24 h, by shares and comments per view, profile visits and follows, not views.
