## Logline
Ross owns a café. A guy buys one small drip coffee at eight and is still on his laptop at three. Every polite hint Ross drops gets an answer: the coffee was bought, the café is empty, and the "someone" who might want the table turns out to be his team. By evening his startup sells for forty million at Ross's tables, and he asks whether refills are free.

## Hooks
1. **Chosen.** Coins slap the counter, a tiny cup lands, "$2.50", "One small drip." The wall clock whirls from eight to three. The guy is still there, cobwebs on him, the same tiny cup. "...Can I get you anything else?" / "I bought a coffee." The label names the business, the clock and the cobwebs name the problem, and the open question is what the owner can do.
2. The cobwebs first: the guy webbed to his chair at 3 PM, then the flashback to the cup at eight. The oddest picture first, the cause second.
3. The peak first: a gong, champagne, "SOLD! FORTY MILLION!" in a café full of laptops. Nobody knows yet why it matters to the owner.

## The screenplay (v1)

INT. ROSS'S CAFÉ - DAY

Big over the first shot: POV: YOU OWN A CAFÉ.

INSERT. The counter. A hand slaps down a few coins, CLINK. Ross's hand sets a tiny paper cup next to them. "$2.50" pops over it.

THE CAMPER (O.S.): One small drip.

INSERT. The wall clock. Its hands whirl from 8:00 to 3:00, WHOOSH. The light through the window swings across the room.

THE CAMPER (late 20s, grey fleece vest over a light blue shirt, white earbuds, a laptop covered in stickers) sits at a table by the window, eyes on the screen. Cobwebs run from his elbow to the chair and from the tiny cup to the laptop. Dust on his hair.

ROSS (the café owner, behind the counter in his brown apron, polite):

ROSS: ...Can I get you anything else?

THE CAMPER (not looking up): I bought a coffee.

ROSS: ...At eight.

The Camper lifts the tiny cup. A cobweb stretches from it to the table. He takes a long sip of nothing.

THE CAMPER: Still drinking it.

ROSS: ...Someone else might want that table.

The Camper waves a hand at the room.

THE CAMPER: Who?

WIDE. The café is empty. Every other table is bare. A tumbleweed rolls across the floor. A cricket chirps.

DING-A-LING. The door opens. Three more guys in the same grey fleece vest and the same earbuds, each with a laptop under his arm, line up at the counter.

GUY 1: One small drip.

GUY 2: Small drip.

GUY 3: ...Just water.

SCRAPE: they shove all the tables together into one long table. Extension cords snake across the floor. A whiteboard rolls in. A ring light. A big brass gong. One cord is plugged into the wall behind the counter: the espresso machine sputters and dies.

Ross holds up the espresso machine's pulled plug, calm.

ROSS: ...When are you leaving?

THE CAMPER (headset on, mid-call): When we sell the company.

INSERT. His laptop screen: OFFER ACCEPTED. $40,000,000.

He bangs the gong. GONNNG.

THE CAMPER: SOLD! FORTY MILLION!

Champagne corks pop. Foam sprays over the counter and all over Ross. He stands there dripping, a polite smile.

The Camper strolls up to the counter, beaming, and holds out the tiny cup.

THE CAMPER: Refills are free, right?

ROSS (dripping, flat): ...Forty million.

The Camper's smile freezes. A beat. He digs in his vest pocket and slaps a few coins on the counter, CLINK.

CUT TO: frame 1. "One small drip." The loop.

**Sounds.** The 8 raw clips were measured (10 ms envelope, peak, spectrum) and cut by those numbers into public/sfx (scripts/sfx-build.py PLAN, src/sfx-peaks.json); nobody has listened to them. What the numbers said:
