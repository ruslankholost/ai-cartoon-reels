# x19 "Screen Time", Part 2 of "Unattended" (script v1, 2026-10-29)

Ross 10-29: "so what about the new video, which one are we making? the kids one actually took off, 10k views". x18 "Unattended" is the best reel since the pilot, so x19 stays in the same café with the same mom and toddler and takes the next parents-against-everyone fight. It goes end to end, from script to the finished render, with no stop for approval in between (Ross's rule for the series since 10-27). Ross stays calm and dry (docs/bible.md, 10-27).

## The real material under it
- Our TikTok search (the idea notes of 10-26, not included here): "ipad kids in restaurant" 33M views over its top 10, "speakerphone in public" 18M (one 2026 video has 209k shares). In 2026 a skit about an entitled mom against a restaurant's iPad reached 4.5M (May).
- The fight: "headphones exist" against "try taking a screen off a three-year-old". The ending hands the parents their point (the silence is worse than the noise), so both sides get ammunition.
- Ours, not sourced: the stroller whose canopy is a TV, the customers who glaze over too, the café turned cinema, the charger for six fifty.

## Logline
Ross owns a café. The mom from Part 1 is back, and her toddler's tablet plays at full volume. Every polite fix Ross tries makes her bring a bigger screen, until the whole café is staring at it like the toddler. Ross turns the lights off and sells popcorn. When the battery dies and the silence is scarier than the noise, he sells her a charger, and it starts again.

## Hooks
1. **Chosen.** The volume bar slams to MAX, the song blasts, and Ross takes it like a hurricane: hair and apron blown back, cups flying off the shelf. The label names the business, the blast names the problem, and the open question is what the owner can do.

3. The peak first: a dark café of glazed adults eating popcorn. The oddest picture, but nobody knows why yet.

## The screenplay (v1)

INT. ROSS'S CAFÉ - DAY

Big over the first shot: POV: YOU OWN A CAFÉ, and under it, small: PART 2.

INSERT. A tablet propped against the sugar jar on a café table, a cartoon sun bouncing on its screen. The volume bar slams to MAX. A squeaky kids' song BLASTS.

ROSS (the café owner, behind the counter in his brown apron) takes the blast like a hurricane: his hair and apron blown straight back, his cheeks rippling, cups flying off the shelf behind him, SMASH. He keeps a polite smile.

The TODDLER (3, wild brown hair, green dinosaur t-shirt) sits at the table, mouth hanging open, eyes glazed and lit blue by the screen. The muffin in front of him is untouched.

ROSS (politely, over the song): ...Could you turn it down a bit?

His MOM (30s, blond messy bun, big sunglasses pushed up on her head, a huge tumbler, a stroller parked beside her) scrolls her own phone without looking up.

MOM: He needs it to eat.

Ross looks at the untouched muffin.

ROSS: ...He's not eating.

MOM: Do you have—

ROSS: ...No.

MOM: Then you wouldn't understand.

Ross reaches over and taps the volume down one notch, TIK. The song dips. The toddler's bottom lip starts to tremble. A tiny whimper.

The mom's head snaps up from her phone.

MOM: No no no no NO!

She slams the volume back up and props her own phone next to the tablet, playing a second song. Twice as loud. The picture frames on the wall rattle.

Ross holds out a pair of tiny kids' headphones, calm.

ROSS: ...Headphones?

The toddler's lip trembles again.

MOM: He HATES headphones!

She yanks the stroller over and flips up its canopy. Inside the canopy is a TV as big as the table. It comes on with a THOOM. The windows bulge with the sound.

One by one, the other CUSTOMERS turn to the giant screen. Their mouths fall open, their eyes glaze blue. Just like the toddler.

Ross looks at his café. He flips the light switch, CLICK: everything goes dark except the blue glow. He puts on a paper usher's hat and holds up a striped popcorn bucket.

ROSS: ...Popcorn?

Every hand in the dark shoots up holding cash. Nobody's eyes leave the screen.

The toddler munches popcorn, eyes on the screen.

MOM (delighted): See? He's eating!

BLIP. A big red 1% on the screen. Black. Silence.

Every face in the café freezes. The toddler's bottom lip begins to tremble.

MOM (a terrified whisper): ...Charger?

Ross slides a charger across the counter.

ROSS: Six fifty.

She slaps down the cash and jams the plug in. The screen lights up and the volume bar slams to MAX.

(The cut to the first shot loops it.)

**Sounds.** The 16 raw clips were measured (envelope at 10 ms, peak, spectrum) and cut by those numbers into public/sfx (scripts/sfx-build.py PLAN, src/sfx-peaks.json); nobody has listened to them. What the numbers say:
- x19-hop is wrong for a muffin: 83 % of its energy is under 60 Hz (the dominant tone is 25 Hz) and its two hits (0.21 and 0.41 s) are sub-bass thumps. It is not used: the three hops use x16's `boing`, 0.25 s of it each, at -3.5, -5 and -5 dB.
- x19-blast (88 % under 120 Hz, dominant 36 Hz), x19-thoom (99 % under 120 Hz, dominant 43 Hz, raw peak -34 dBFS: levelled up 31 dB) and x19-windows (72 % under 120 Hz) are sub-bass booms that a phone speaker, which plays nothing under about 150 Hz, will hardly reproduce (x12's thunder and x09's pyro are the same kind and went out that way). Each has a partner in the middle range: the thoom has x10's `whump` (58 % at 120-250 Hz) on its hit, the blast has x10's `whoosh` (it starts on the blast's frame at -3 dB and swells to its peak 0.10 s in) and the windows have x11's `rattle` (the glass shaking in its frame: it starts on the windows' frame, runs 0.7 s, +2 dB); the song carries the loudness.
